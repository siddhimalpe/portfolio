Schools teach money as abstract math, while families avoid discussing it until adulthood. Without a safe space to experience financial trade-offs, young adults only discover the consequences of poor financial habits when their mistakes start costing real money and real debt

Who are we designing for?

Teenagers / pre-teens aged 10-16

Want independence but don't always understand the consequences of financial decisions.

(02) WHY CURRENT SOLUTIONS FAIL

Traditional Board Games

Gameplay relies on dice rolls & luck

Encourage predatory hoarding & bankrupting peers

Teach zero real-world budgetary cause & effect

Teen Neo-Banks

Prepaid debit cards tied to real currency

High parental hesitation to grant minors financial independence

Without built-in habits, teens just use cards for fast food

(03) KEY INSIGHTS

Primary research 23 teens + 7 parents Remote interviews Sentence completion

Pre-teens and teens (10-16) want financial independence, but parents mostly give money only when needed, which creates tension between freedom and control.

These teens understand basic ideas like saving and needs vs wants, but struggle with real-world finance, topics like taxes, interest, and investing feel confusing & advanced.

They rely on memory or piggy banks to track money, which leads to forgetting spends, running out unexpectedly & feeling stressed.

They are strongly motivated to earn and grow money, but they engage only when learning feels interactive & game-like rather than lecture-based.

(04) PERSONA

Handles his own spending but parents set the limits

Gets pocket money from parents + occasional festival cash

Curious about money but has never been taught how it actually works

Owns a smartphone but supervised

Arjun, 14

"I know the terms... but not what they actually mean."

Goals

  • Feel independent with money without asking parents for permission every time
  • Understand real financial terms (EMI, investing) instead of just memorizing definitions
  • Not run out of money without knowing why

Frustrations

  • Tracks spending in his head / piggy bank, forgets what he spent & panics when numbers don't add up
  • Existing finance apps & videos feel like lectures, will not choose to do for fun
  • Caught between wanting freedom and his parents wanting oversight, creates mild annoyance
  • Investing/credit/EMI sound "advanced" and intimidating, so he avoids engaging with them at all

Behaviour

  • Learns fastest when something feels "real life but fun”
  • Plays games with friends already, more likely to learn finance through play than through solo app use
  • Will disengage the moment something feels like a lesson

(08) SOLUTION

Introducing

A phygital experience pairing Jenga with generative AI scenario. Every money decision dictates the tower's physical balance: smart choices keep it steady, while reckless spending forces shaky placements until it falls. Dilemmas scale across three life stages - school, teen years, and college.

(09) GAMEPLAY LOOP

Pull a Block

Every block has a number printed on it. Pull one out and choose the number into the app

This input triggers the situation

Face a dilemma

The app’s AI writes a money decision that fits your age and the block you pulled.

You read the options and pick one

Reckless Choice?
Place the block UPRIGHT
Balanced Compromise?
Place the block SIDEWAYS
Good Financial Decision?
Place the block FLAT

Place the block

You read the options and pick one

The app rates your choice and tells you how to place the block on your tower

Collapse & endgame

Bad decisions pile up, and eventually the tower falls. The app calculates every player’s FHS (Financial Health Score) by how healthy their budget is

The player with highest FHS wins
