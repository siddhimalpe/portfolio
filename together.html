(02) Research Question, Hypothesis

Under what social, environmental, & psychological conditions do boys and girls (ages 8-12) feel safe enough to engage in mixed-gender collaboration, instead of defaulting to exclusion?

(03) Hypothesis

Children do not avoid the opposite gender because of dislike, but because of fear of peer judgement; reducing social sanctions will unlock their willingness to collaborate.

(04) Research Method

We gave the participants research toolkits: activities to draw, finish and fill in, so they show us what they think without having to say it out loud

(05) Research Progression in an Iceberg

Events

Observational Study

• 9 Participants • MITID Evening School

Observed organic play patterns to measure physical distance, & and what triggers self-segregation

Physical Hesitation

Children showed immediate physical hesitation: avoiding eye contact, freezing up, & instinctively retreating to same-gender comfort zones

  • no eye contact
  • avoiding direct interaction
  • physical distancing
  • refusal to sit/pair
  • verbal unacceptance of opp. gender
  • taunting/mocking opp. gender
But why are they staying apart?
Patterns

Research Toolkit 01

• 36 Participants • Army Public School

Used story-completion exercises to find out why they stay apart and which peer norms are behind it

Vocal Group Performance

In front of their peers, exclusion became a loud, active performance, children policed one another to prove loyalty & protect their social standing

  • go quiet in mixed groups
  • let in only if good at the task
  • pressure each other to stay apart
  • split up without being asked
  • mix only while a teacher watches
But what is influencing these beliefs?
Structures

Research Toolkit 02

• 14 Participants • Take home booklets

Focused on attitudes using 3-day individual booklets to extract unmasked thoughts away from peers.

Fear of Teasing

Children didn't dislike the other gender at all, they were just terrified that friendship would be teased as "romance"

  • privately want to mix
  • fear being teased
  • friendship gets called “romance”
  • act differently when friends watch
How did this become a habit every child follows
Mental Models
From analysis across all three studies

Systemic Conditioning

Seating, routines & adult language made separation look normal. Children learned following it was safe, & mixing needed someone's approval

  • adults make separating feel normal
  • staying apart avoids conflict
  • kids excuse exclusion as "fair play"
  • mixing requires group permission
  • playing by the rules keeps a child safe

Beneath the surface,
children aren’t rejecting each other,
they’re protecting themselves from judgment, teasing, and the
unsaid rules that make mixed-gender interaction feel unsafe.

(06) Key Insights

Girls want to cross into boys’ dominated spaces because they’re aspirational. Boys know, & aggressively defend these spaces

Collaboration is currency. Children will cooperate across genders if there is payoff or reward

When an adult is watching, children say and do the “right” thing. When the adult steps away, they go back to self-segregate

Which activities & friendships count as “acceptable” is policed by parents, siblings, grandparents, teachers & friends

Children often say they’d be friends with the other gender but avoid doing it, they’ll draw or imagine one, yet won’t sit, pair or play with one

(08) Solution

Together is a toolkit for teachers. It uses behaviour-building rituals to slowly change how children interact with the opposite gender.

PRESSURE SHIFTS

No child has to choose to include anyone, because the rule does it for them. Inclusion stops being a social risk and becomes part of the classroom routine. When a dominant child follows the ritual, the others follow too.

Toolkit Box

A classroom box split into daily, weekly, and monthly activities

The activity is in pairs, so inclusion becomes automatic & no one questions it

Instructions are inside the lid, takes under 5 minutes to conduct

Ritual Design

Quick warm-ups to get boys & girls talking so it stops feeling awkward

Hands-on team tasks where two kids have to cooperate to get it done

Makes mixed-gender interaction rule-based, repeated, and normal.

What’s Inside the Kits?

The kit is one box with three compartments Daily, Weekly and Monthly Each one holds everything that cycle needs, so the teacher can use it straight from the box

Daily

3-5 minute prompts that remove daily awkwardness and make talking to each other fun

Weekly

Hands-on team tasks that require active cooperation and build real familiarity

Monthly

Milestone rituals to reflect on shared progress and lock in long-term habits

Who uses the kit?

Intervention Entry Point

The first 3 to 5 minutes of class or during desk transitions, the exact moment when children choose their seats and split by default

Teachers

Educators who want an integrated classroom without giving moral lectures, policing breaks, or adding prep to their day

Children

Kids who pull away out of peer fear, not dislike, & need explicit classroom rules that give them safe cover to interact

How we’d know it’s working

A 6 - 10 week run in one classroom, starting with a baseline week to observe normal seating and play patterns before using the kit

Success Metrics

↑ Mixed-gender pairs form without the teacher prompting

↓ Hesitation or resistance when pairs are assigned

↓ Teacher steps in less each week

↑ Spillover: children mix on their own outside the ritual
